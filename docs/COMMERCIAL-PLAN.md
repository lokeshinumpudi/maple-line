# From playable chapter to a saleable game

## The promise

A small narrative railway game about Haru Morita, sixty, taking his granddaughter Emi through villages he has served for forty-one years. The player drives, listens, performs station duties, and decides what to ask. The railway should feel inhabited through specific recurring people and their work.

The current build is a browser prototype with eighteen authored conversations across five chapters, choice-dependent notebook memories, a saved narrative and station duty, character-specific synthetic voices, Haru and Emi plus five village residents, and two persistent object tasks. It is not yet a commercial release. Generated reference illustrations are included; the in-world models are procedural stylized models, not meshes generated from those images.

## Revenue target

The requested $10,000 is a target, not a forecast. At a hypothetical $15 list price, 667 full-price sales produce $10,005 of gross sales before storefront deductions, discounts, refunds, taxes, and development costs. Price and demand are unvalidated. Net receipts of $10,000 require a separate calculation with actual storefront terms and costs.

No storefront account, fee, public listing, or paid promotion has been created by this work.

## Release gates

- A complete start-to-ending playthrough with no lost progress, dead ends, or unsafe railway state transitions.
- At least one physical player-operated junction and three distinct adventures, rather than repeating a dialogue and boarding checklist at every stop.
- Art consistency between illustrations, characters, train, vegetation, water, and station props. Character faces and poses must remain readable in the actual game view.
- Human editorial and cultural review of the fictional Japanese setting, names, dialogue, and railway practices. The game is stylized fiction, not an operational training simulator.
- Readable subtitles, keyboard-only play, reduced motion, independent voice/ambience levels, and rebindable controls. Synthetic narration remains optional; it is not a replacement for a reviewed voice performance.
- Saves covering narrative, duties, location, and settings; migration and recovery tests before changing the campaign data format.
- Measured performance on at least an integrated-graphics laptop and a representative midrange desktop, including rain, snow, the city, and character close-ups. Aim for a sustained 60 fps at the selected quality level; report frame-time percentiles, not only an average.
- A packaged offline desktop build with asset credits, crash recovery, and a tested first-run flow. Browser development mode and agent tools are not the retail interface.
- Playtests with people who have not read the design documents. Observe whether they understand the first objective, remain interested through a full chapter, and want to continue. Fix observed problems before claiming commercial readiness.

## Distribution work after those gates

Prepare a short demo, actual gameplay trailer, screenshots, store copy, and a support channel. Measure demo completion and wishlisting before spending on promotion. Steam currently documents a $100 Direct fee per app, recoupable after $1,000 in adjusted gross revenue, and a minimum two-week Coming Soon period before release. Consult the current official requirements before submitting or paying anything: [Steam Direct fee](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee), [Coming Soon](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon), [Steam events](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events).

Do not equate an automated playthrough, generated artwork, or an attractive screenshot with evidence of demand. The next evidence should come from players completing the demo and choosing to follow the game.
