# Japanese railway and village life: research for the next story draft

Research date: **21 September 2026**. This note supports an original fictional railway, not a reconstruction of any cited place. Dates below describe the source or the event, not necessarily current service. Translations and summaries are mine. No passenger testimony below should be copied into a fictional character’s mouth or attributed to that character as lived experience.

I reviewed the current `story-data.js` and `STORY-BIBLE.md`. Their cast and affectionate humour are worth retaining. Their main weakness is causal: almost every stop supplies an anecdote, then leaves the next stop unchanged. The radio programme gives the pair something to collect but little that they must decide, repair, or disagree about in the present.

## Ten findings and their level implications

### 1. A single-track token is authority for a specific occupied section

**Primary source:** [JR East Chiba Branch, Kururi Line tablet-system retirement announcement](https://www.jreast.co.jp/chiba/news/pdf/20120127tabureto.pdf), **27 January 2012**, PDF page 1. It describes cooperation between the staff at both ends of a block to release one tablet for the driver. It also announces replacement by a different automatic block system on **17 March 2012** because of equipment ageing.

**Usable detail:** A tablet is not a generic key, souvenir, or permission to choose any branch. The source is historical; it does not establish that contemporary regional railways universally use tablets.

**Level implication:** At Momiji, show the opposing train’s occupation, the agreed route, and the actual release of authority. Waiting is skilled work. If Maple Line keeps modern signals, call the gameplay item a section authority, or explicitly make physical tablets a heritage operating system. Never imply Haru can bypass it because he knows everyone.

### 2. Fifteen minutes can change whether a school day is workable

**Primary source:** [Kimitsu City, third regional public transport meeting papers](https://www.city.kimitsu.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/57993.pdf), **22 December 2025**, PDF pages 8–9 and the attached user survey. The city reports student objections to a proposed **05:45** first replacement-bus departure and says it will move the departure later, considering trial-run results.

**Usable detail:** A timetable consultation can produce a precise amendment. The issue is not simply “young people do not use the railway.”

**Level implication:** Emi’s future can depend on an identifiable connection. Let the player compare her course’s finishing time, the connecting service, and the last village bus. An adult’s “you can always come home” should be tested against those times.

### 3. Waiting needs a roof and a seat

**Primary source:** The same [Kimitsu December 2025 meeting papers](https://www.city.kimitsu.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/57993.pdf), PDF page 9, report plans to coordinate shelters and benches at principal replacement-bus stops, reflecting local wishes.

**Usable detail:** A replacement route’s existence does not answer how someone waits for it.

**Level implication:** Give a wet transfer point a visible, limited sheltered space. A scene can concern whether a bench is reachable with shopping, rather than a monologue about loneliness. Do not label a bus solution inherently inferior: show the particular transfer problem.

### 4. A hospital trip can depend on a four-minute connection

**Primary source:** [Nanto City newsletter, volume 231](https://www.city.nanto.toyama.jp/material/files/group/3/0000090256.pdf), **February 2024**, municipal-bus information item. After suspension of a first bus, the city described extra Monday/Thursday service reaching Jōhana Station at **08:40**, connecting with an **08:44** hospital-bound bus.

**Usable detail:** Different operators and service days form one resident’s trip. These are historical published times, not current travel advice.

**Level implication:** Kawasemi needs a connection board and a passenger with a destination beyond the railway station. The dispatch interaction should be “confirm whether the connection can wait,” not “drive faster despite restrictions.” The outcome can be an arranged alternative rather than a medical emergency.

### 5. Young residents can want both a career elsewhere and a future for home

**Primary source:** The same [Nanto February 2024 newsletter](https://www.city.nanto.toyama.jp/material/files/group/3/0000090256.pdf), printed page 3, interviews residents turning twenty. Their ambitions include media work, education, public service, travel, and changes they would like at home.

**Usable detail:** These are individual answers selected for a municipal feature, not representative findings about all Japanese young people.

**Level implication:** Emi should have a specific sound-course application, an unfinished recording she wants to improve, and friends outside this story. Wanting to leave need not mean rejecting Haru, nor should loving him require staying.

### 6. Restoring a service takes a driver and continuing local work

**Primary source:** [Chiba City, Daiganji bus-route liaison group](https://www.city.chiba.jp/toshi/toshi/kotsu/daiganzisentiikirennrakukai.html), updated **19 June 2026**. The page says weekend service resumed on **14 June 2025** after recruiting a driver and agreeing community promotion. A liaison group continues reviewing use and problems.

**Usable detail:** This is a city bus case, not proof about every rural railway. It demonstrates a concrete service constraint and a collective response.

**Level implication:** Haru’s availability has a cost. He cannot solve a roster shortage by promising himself to every shift indefinitely. Fumi can insist that a viable plan has named cover and days off, while Keiko asks which family commitments he has already volunteered away.

### 7. Snow can become more dangerous when the weather improves

**Primary source:** [JR East Niigata Branch, Tadami Line operating plan](https://www.jreast.co.jp/aas/20250305_20250306_ni01.pdf), **6 March 2025**. It announced suspension beginning 7 March because snow clearance was difficult and rising temperatures brought falling-snow and avalanche risks. It explicitly said no substitute bus service would run for that suspension.

**Usable detail:** A blue sky does not itself establish that the route is safe. This notice concerns one event and section.

**Level implication:** The mountain level should show snow sheds, clearance work, and a dispatch inspection result. Haru’s competence is accepting a hold and communicating it. Do not reward racing through a closure; do not assume a bus is automatically available as a fallback.

### 8. Disruption changes capacity and passenger instructions, not only scenery

**Primary source:** [Kominato Railway, commuter and student guidance](https://www.kominato.co.jp/guide/commute/), operational notice updated **20 September 2026**, checked 21 September. The notice describes rain-damage replacement buses/taxis with different departure times and school-travel priority on replacement taxis, which may fill. The same page explains buying tickets from a conductor where a station has no available ticket staff and says IC cards cannot be used on this railway.

**Usable detail:** These are operator-specific arrangements and a changing live notice. Do not transplant them as national rules.

**Level implication:** A rain chapter can ask the player to provide the right instruction and identify capacity before making a promise. Ticket handling can be a short social encounter, with no assumption that an older passenger is technologically confused.

### 9. A working sixty-year-old needs ordinary motives, not automatic frailty

**Primary source:** [Cabinet Office, 2025 Annual Report on the Ageing Society, employment section](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_3_1.html). Its underlying national survey covered adults **60 and over**, conducted **1 October–8 November 2024**, with **2,188 valid responses**. Among respondents in paid work, income was the most commonly stated main reason; using experience and skills was a prominent reason for selecting a job.

**Usable detail:** Group survey results neither define Haru nor justify assumptions about health, gender, retirement, or family structure.

**Level implication:** Haru can like his work, need the wage, and fear becoming replaceable at the same time. Keiko must also have work and preferences. Their disagreement should concern this household’s choices rather than a claim about “Japanese family duty.”

### 10. Maintaining service can still mean charging residents more

**Primary source:** [Echigo Tokimeki Railway, fare-revision announcement](https://www.echigo-tokimeki.co.jp/information/detail?id=2397), **1 September 2025**, describing a revision effective **1 October 2025**. The operator states an average **18%** revision and says planned public support would not by itself reverse its difficult financial position.

**Usable detail:** This is one operator’s explanation of a particular revision. It is neither a universal rural-railway condition nor an independent assessment of its finances.

**Level implication:** Nao can support the line while objecting to a specific delivery cost. Supporting public transport does not require every resident to accept every proposal. Let her bakery have a ledger, an order deadline, and a reasonable alternative.

## A stronger fictional conflict to test

**Proposal, not a fact drawn from these sources:** Haru has endorsed a proposed winter timetable because it makes the single-track service and staff roster easier to operate. Emi is recording one working day for the local radio programme. She discovers the proposal breaks the evening feeder connection she would need after her city course. Keiko has already been filling that gap for other family trips in her car; Haru has counted those lifts as available without asking her.

This puts the railway, Haru’s competence, Emi’s ambition, and Keiko’s time in the same conflict. Haru has not maliciously cut a village off. His mistake is narrower and believable: he mistook the part of the transport system he understands for the whole of someone else’s day.

Fumi’s spanner and the notebook can remain, but they stop carrying all the emotional weight. The spanner reveals his habit of treating someone else’s availability as an indefinite loan. Nao’s bread order gives the new timetable an economic consequence. Neither woman exists to deliver a lesson and disappear; each should ask for a specific practical answer.

Emi should not arrive with the complete correct answer. Her first edited recording may remove hesitation and make a resident’s qualified opinion sound like opposition to the railway. Haru challenges that fairly. She then asks permission to use a fuller passage. Both learn something about representing other people.

The ending need not save or close the line. Haru can withdraw his blanket endorsement and submit a small, costed correction: a protected connection on specified days, a transfer shelter, and named roster cover. Other people decide with him. His personal commitment is equally specific: he will attend Emi’s open day without volunteering Keiko as his substitute, and he will ask his son about the concert rather than narrate it again.

Possible exchange, **new fictional writing**:

> Haru: “I made sure the last train got everyone back.”
>
> Keiko: “To the station. I drove the rest.”
>
> Haru: “You could have told me it was too much.”
>
> Keiko: “I’m telling you I had somewhere to go.”

## Sources and interpretation limits

- The best direct community material here is local-government consultation and a municipal youth interview feature. I did **not** find and verify an oral history of a sixty-year-old Japanese railway grandfather that would justify imitating a real person’s family life.
- One older Kimitsu meeting attachment (`52229.pdf`) appeared in search with useful 2023 resident statements but returned 404 when opened. Its claims are not used as evidence above. The live December 2025 meeting packet supports the narrower claims retained here.
- All sources are operators or government bodies. Operators explain their own decisions; official surveys have sampling and selection limits. A character may reasonably disagree with an operator without the story declaring either side dishonest.
- No source establishes Maple Line’s exact ticket system, authority procedure, 4% grade operation, snowy elevation, radio programme, or delivery contract. Those require explicit fictional design and, for a paid release making realism claims, review by people familiar with the relevant railway work and Japanese language.
- Narrative depth is not achieved by adding disaster, dementia, abandonment, or death. The research supports ordinary competing needs: time, money, work, access, and the right to make plans of one’s own.
