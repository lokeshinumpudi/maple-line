## Diagnosis of the current campaign

The premise already has useful specificity: Haru is sixty and still working; Emi is seventeen and interested in sound; Keiko has her own work; the railway connects a bakery, workshop, growers, and café. Keep those features, the humour, and the absence of a compulsory illness or threatened village extinction.

The main weaknesses are structural:

1. **Most choices stop at their immediate response.** `bread-mother`, `paddy-work`, and `recital-own` produce different keepsakes, but the next principal scene is largely unchanged. The player can choose curiosity repeatedly without another person recognizing it. Recent wildlife recording callbacks are an exception worth extending.
2. **The important wound arrives late and resolves verbally.** At `kiri-missed-recital`, Haru admits that work was easier than uncertainty at home. At `hoshimi-emi-plan`, both replies are already emotionally mature. The player has not had to make a present-day commitment resembling the old mistake.
3. **Emi’s application has little preparation.** Her city plan appears after fourteen kilometres. Her earlier actions mainly give Haru questions to answer. Seed her competence, private work, and objections before the mountain.
4. **People function mostly as examples of a theme.** Nao, Yuta, and Mika describe contemporary livelihoods, but their transactions are rarely visible and their needs rarely alter the service. Give the player one linked delivery and make its arrival visible.
5. **The finale asserts work the player did not do.** The programme is declared assembled and sent. There is no edit, consent decision, playback, or delivery action proving it exists. Recent wildlife capture supplies a start, not the whole production.
6. **Every scene uses the same stop-and-read rhythm.** The current arrival engine requires a slow train for dialogue. Applying that equally to bridge and tunnel observations turns a continuous railway ride into eighteen similar pauses. The bridge should be heard while crossing; the tunnel should change sound and visibility while the train continues. This is a game-design recommendation, not an assertion about operating rules on a real Japanese railway.
7. **The visual claims exceed the staged scenes.** Returning a spanner, handing over bread, and noticing a shared seat often happen only in prose. The art workload should follow those actions before adding more anonymous buildings.

The story bible’s scene table also still places the Momiji bread beat at Z190, while the source places it at Z525. Treat the source as the current location until documentation is synchronized.

## Recommended dramatic spine

**Haru’s mistaken belief:** if people need him to keep working, they will know he cares. If another driver can do the job, he fears the sacrifices he made for it will look unnecessary.

**Emi’s desire:** make a recording that sounds like the people she knows now, then take her interest in sound beyond the village. She wants Haru to listen before volunteering a future for her.

**Present-day pressure:** Haru is asked to confirm the return-service plan. A qualified relief driver, Rin, can cover it. Emi also has a specific plan for recording the harbour with Haru. He cannot indefinitely answer both people with “we’ll see.” The radio move remains today’s small, finishable public project.

**The central decision:** at Hoshimi, commit to one of two agreed plans, then carry it out at Minato:

- **Mentor the return service.** Haru keeps a previously discussed mentoring commitment with Rin. He tells Emi early, helps her prepare to record independently, and puts a concrete later outing in the calendar. The player performs the return briefing and drives its opening section with Rin’s observations.
- **Accept the relief and ride with Emi.** Haru completes a proper handover to the qualified driver, takes the passenger seat, and lets Emi lead the harbour recording. The player checks the handover, then operates the recorder and chooses where to listen while the service continues under its assigned driver.

Neither option cancels passenger service, treats Emi’s independence as rejection, or makes breaking safety rules a loving act. Both have a cost: Haru misses a different experience with someone he values. Rin must be introduced before the choice as a capable colleague with an existing commitment, not appear at the last minute as a moral test. Emi must explicitly accept the independent plan; the game cannot label an imposed absence “respecting independence.”

The dramatic change is **making a specific promise and following through**, rather than always selecting the kindest-sounding line. Haru’s son does not instantly forgive him because the player completed a task. A later message can acknowledge the invitation without resolving their history.

This adds one character and two short final activities. If the immediate implementation budget cannot stage Rin and a passenger recording interaction, do not claim this branch exists. Build the linked bread delivery and a real recording/edit action first, then keep the central decision marked as planned.

## Runtime and writing changes needed

Use a small set of named facts, not a hidden kindness total:

- `returnPlan`: unset, mentor, or ride; chosen once and saved.
- `recordings`: acquired clip IDs, source person, permission, and optional quality description. Performance must not make a deaf player’s story incomplete.
- `deliveries`: bread and bench parcel pickup/arrival states; repeat actions must be idempotent.
- `corrections`: specific remembered details, including whether Fumi told the bicycle story herself.
- `familyContact`: invitation sent and response received; do not equate a response with forgiveness.

Add explicit scene delivery types: **moving observation**, **platform conversation**, **station task**, and **optional quiet view**. Long choices remain at safe, authored stops. Moving observations may be paused or replayed, and their content remains in the notebook if controls require attention. “Continue to next memory” must not manufacture completed cargo, permission, or recorded audio.

For dialogue, separate spoken lines by character and attach a short performance intention. A paragraph containing Emi’s question and Haru’s narration should not be sent to one voice as a finished scene. Increase the number of exchanges only where a character resists, misunderstands, or revises something. Do not lengthen every card.

Author callbacks as short variations at shared scenes. Preserve the same railway route and most scene geometry. Save the selected return plan and task facts; changing the script requires a migration or an explicit compatibility message, not silently discarding existing notebooks.

## First implementation slice and evidence

Build **Momiji → bridge → summit delivery** first. It proves that one person, one object, and one sound can survive across several locations. Then add the return-service commitment and its two final activities. This sequence tests continuity before funding eighteen bespoke scenes.

Acceptance checks for that slice:

1. A new player can explain what Nao needs and what Emi is making without reading a design document.
2. Boarding, waiting, and departing alter the delivery state through actual game events. A scene does not claim success after a skipped task.
3. At the summit, the player recognizes the same crate and hears a response tied to their earlier encounter.
4. The bridge remains a moving scenic interval, with replayable dialogue and no compulsory text pause in its middle.
5. The final edit cannot use an unrecorded or private clip. Captioned playback communicates the same choices as sound.
6. Each return-plan playthrough shows a different final activity, a later callback, and an acknowledged cost. Both can finish without a relationship score or catastrophic failure.
7. Record observed confusion, skipped dialogue, task retries, and remembered characters in small external playtests. Passing unit tests verifies state transitions; it does not prove emotional depth or commercial demand.

These are proposed evaluation criteria, not results already achieved.
