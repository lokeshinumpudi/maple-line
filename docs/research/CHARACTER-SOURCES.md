# Where to get characters and animation

Research note, 23 September 2026. Nothing here is implemented, and no asset has been downloaded. Licence pages were read on this date. Where a page could not be read (several vendors block automated fetches), the claim is marked **unverified** or names the secondary source it came from. Licences change: re-read the licence page on the day you download anything and keep a copy (see [Provenance](#what-to-record-for-every-character-asset)).

## Short answer

- **Cast bodies:** build them in Blender with **MPFB2** (the MakeHuman add-on). Its output is CC0, it covers ages from child to elderly with East Asian proportions, and it can be scripted, which matches the build-script rule in the [Blender asset skill](../../.agents/skills/maple-blender-assets/SKILL.md). Clothes, uniforms and hair are modelled by us in the same scripts, so every figure shares one look.
- **Animation:** **Quaternius Universal Animation Library 1 and 2** (CC0, glTF, one humanoid rig), topped up with **Mesh2Motion** and **KayKit** clips (both CC0). Retarget once, offline, onto the single Maple Line skeleton and hand-key the clips no library has (`check-phone`, `watch-train`, `shelter`, `board`).
- **Pipeline test first:** drop in a free Quaternius or KayKit character before the real cast exists, to prove the loader, mixer, crowd drawing and director portrait on a phone.
- **Avoid for this project:** TurboSquid and CGTrader (a plain GLB served to a browser conflicts with their protection clauses), Hunyuan3D (licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, including display of outputs), free tiers of Meshy/Tripo (attribution or non-commercial), Ready Player Me (shut down 31 January 2026), and Mixamo files in the public repository.

## What Maple Line needs from a source

- **Licence:** the source repository `lokeshinumpudi/maple-line` is public (checked through the GitHub API today), and the game is published on a personal site, with commercial release possible. Anything committed is redistributed to everyone as a raw file. The repository has no `LICENSE` file, so the terms for its own files are also undefined; third-party files keep their own licence.
- **Style:** restrained and stylized, matching the gouache reference in [CHARACTERS.md](../CHARACTERS.md) ("natural proportions, no chibi"). Photoreal faces risk looking uncanny under the director's portrait shots; anime faces clash with the reference art.
- **Cast range:** students, commuters, shopkeepers, gardeners, elders (`MIND_ROLES` in `simulation/npc-minds.js`), station staff and school uniforms, and seasonal layers (rain, snow).
- **Motion:** clips named after the NPC intents. The current `INTENTS` list is `continue`, `linger`, `chat`, `hurry`, `shelter`, `watch-train`, `wave`, `sit`, `check-phone`, `stretch`; the asset skill adds `idle`, `walk` and `board`.
- **Budgets (from the asset skill):** hero characters about 8,000 triangles, crowd characters about 1,500, textures 1024 px (512 for crowd). Around 25 people at Momiji and 8 per regional station, drawn with instanced meshes, on phones.
- **Faces:** expressions for close shots, driven by the nine `MOODS` (`content`, `cheerful`, `wistful`, `anxious`, `impatient`, `curious`, `tired`, `irritated`, `shy`).

### AI generation for characters

Assessment, not a measurement: current image-to-3D and text-to-3D tools produce dense triangulated meshes with lighting baked into the texture, soft or merged facial features, and fused clothing. They need retopology, UV and texture cleanup, and rigging before they fit an 8,000-triangle hero budget. Faces are the weakest part, which is the exact part the director portrait shows. Getting thirty figures with matching proportions and shading from separate prompts is hard. The US Copyright Office's [January 2025 report](https://copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf) says "purely AI-generated material" is not copyrightable, so a commercial release could not stop others copying unedited AI characters. Use AI output for reference sheets or background props, and record the tool, plan and date.

## Licence risks, stated plainly

1. **Mixamo files cannot go in the public repository.** Adobe's FAQ allows use in games but forbids distributing "the raw character and animation files". A GLB of Mixamo clips committed to a public GitHub repository is a raw file anyone can download under whatever terms the repository implies. The official FAQ page blocked fetching today, so this reading rests on Adobe's text as quoted on the Adobe community forum and on third-party summaries. Mixamo has also had long outages (June 2025) and no public roadmap ([Cinevva, 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-character-animations-rigging)). Use it to study motion, not to ship.
2. **TurboSquid and CGTrader licences do not fit a Three.js game.** Both require the model to be protected from extraction. Maple Line serves GLB files that the browser downloads as-is. TurboSquid names only Unity, Unreal, Lumberyard and Stingray WebGL exports.
3. **Hunyuan3D outputs may not be displayed in the EU, UK or South Korea.** A public website is displayed there.
4. **Free AI tiers:** Meshy free output requires "Model created with Meshy – CC BY 4.0 License" credit and Meshy owns it; Tripo free output is reported as non-commercial. Generate only on a paid plan if the result may ship, and keep the receipt and date: rights follow the plan at generation time.
5. **CC-BY and share-alike:** CC-BY needs a visible credit in the game and the repository. CC-BY-SA and GPL art from OpenGameArt would require releasing our modified version under the same licence. NC excludes the commercial plan. ND forbids the edits every asset needs (retopology, rigging).
6. **Mixed licences inside one tool:** MPFB core output is CC0, but some MakeHuman asset packs (Hair 02–03, Shirts 02–03, Suits 03 and others) are CC-BY. VRoid content is licensed, not CC0, and individual items can carry special clauses. Record the licence of each part, not only the tool.
7. **Per-character and per-product licences:** Reallusion Standard covers "one single character", and games need the Extended licence; Daz needs an Interactive License per product, where offered. A 30-person cast multiplies these.
8. **MIT still needs a notice.** Rocketbox (and TRELLIS code) require keeping the copyright and licence text with copies.
9. **Services disappear.** Ready Player Me stopped on 31 January 2026, and integrations stopped working. Sketchfab changed owner in August 2026 and has said it would give notice before ending free downloads. Keep local copies of source files and licence text; never load characters from a third-party service at runtime.

## Technical fit for Three.js r180

- **Format:** glTF/GLB with skins, animations and morph targets loads through `GLTFLoader`. Mixamo, Rocketbox, Daz and Reallusion ship FBX, which means a Blender conversion step. VRM loads through `GLTFLoader` with `VRMLoaderPlugin`. Compress in the export step (meshopt geometry, KTX2 textures) and measure the decode cost on a phone.
- **One skeleton for the whole cast:** in Three.js an `AnimationClip` targets bones by name, so every character that shares bone names can share clips. MPFB's `game_engine` rig is a good candidate (its `rig.game_engine.json` ships in the repository). Retarget library clips onto it once, offline, and commit the baked result. `SkeletonUtils.retargetClip(target, source, clip, options)` takes a `names` bone map, `hip`, `scale`, `fps` and `trim`, so it can run in a Node build script as well as in the browser; runtime retargeting is not needed. UAL 1, UAL 2, KayKit, Mesh2Motion and Mixamo each use a different skeleton, so each needs a bone map. three-vrm's `humanoidAnimation` example (`mixamoVRMRigMap.js`) is a worked bone map.
- **Crowds with skinned characters.** In r180 `SkinnedMesh` and `InstancedMesh` are separate classes: a skinned figure cannot be drawn with the stock `InstancedMesh`, and the `BatchedMesh` source has no skinning path. Options, cheapest first:
  1. Keep the crowd as rigid parts on instanced meshes (today's approach), with better shapes and a few per-instance morph targets. r180 `InstancedMesh.setMorphAt` gives each instance its own morph weights, enough for breathing, arm raises and head turns.
  2. Vertex animation textures: bake each crowd clip to a texture of vertex positions and play it in the vertex shader of one instanced mesh, with a per-instance clip and time offset. Memory grows with vertex count × frames, which is acceptable at 1,500 triangles and short loops.
  3. [`@three.ez/instanced-mesh`](https://github.com/agargaro/instanced-mesh) (InstancedMesh2, MIT, 0.3.16) adds per-instance skinning, LOD and shadow LOD; its demo claims 3,000 skinned instances. Not tested here on phones.
  4. Ordinary `SkinnedMesh` + `AnimationMixer` only for the few people the director frames, swapped in when the camera is near and swapped back out afterwards.
- **Level of detail:** hero skinned mesh within portrait range, crowd mesh beyond it, and nothing (or an impostor card) at distance. The swap must keep the same clothes colours so the cut is not visible.
- **Faces:** for close shots use morph targets (glTF shape keys) on the hero head only. Name them after the VRM 1.0 expression presets (`happy`, `angry`, `sad`, `relaxed`, `surprised`, `aa`, `ih`, `ou`, `ee`, `oh`, `blink`, `blinkLeft`, `blinkRight`) so the naming is documented elsewhere, then map the nine `MOODS` to weighted mixes. MPFB's repository ships 34 face-unit targets per ethnicity (eye closure, brow raise, mouth corner puller and others) under `data/targets/expression/units`; turning them into exported shape keys is **unverified** and needs a Blender test. Rocketbox and VRoid have ready expression sets but the wrong look.
- **Budgets:** Quaternius base characters average about 13k triangles and need reduction even for heroes. MPFB bodies must be rebuilt as low-poly versions for the crowd. Measure with `measure_game_performance` before and after, as the asset skill requires.

## Style: getting one look across the cast

No single free pack covers Maple Line's cast: Quaternius and KayKit have no elders or school uniforms, MakeHuman clothing is generic Western, Rocketbox and Daz are photoreal, VRoid is anime. Consistency therefore has to come from our own pipeline, not from a store.

- One base mesh and one skeleton for everyone (MPFB), varied only by age, height and weight sliders set in the build script.
- One head template with simplified features and painted eyes and brows, tuned against the gouache reference and checked in a portrait shot at daylight and dusk.
- Clothes modelled by us as script-built parts: station staff jacket and cap, gakuran and sailor-style school uniforms, farm and shop aprons, rain capes and umbrellas, winter coats and scarves. Seasonal variants are swapped parts on the same body.
- One material model: flat colour or vertex colour with a shared palette atlas, like the current figures, so crowds keep few draw calls.
- A turnaround sheet per character (front, side, back) rendered by the build script and compared side by side before export.

## Recommended path

1. **Pipeline test (days, not weeks).** Load one Quaternius Universal Base Character with UAL clips (all CC0) into a scratch branch. Prove: GLB loading with fallback, `AnimationMixer` driven by NPC intents, a director portrait, crowd drawing by one of the options above, and phone frame times. Nothing from this step needs to ship.
2. **Base cast in MPFB2.** Script four to six body presets (student, adult, elder; male and female) on the `game_engine` rig. Make a low-poly crowd version of each.
3. **Clothes, hair and faces in Blender scripts**, following the style rules above. Add the expression shape keys to hero heads.
4. **Animation library.** Retarget UAL 1 and UAL 2 clips (idle, walk, jog as `hurry`, sit, wave, talk as `chat`, stretch-like emotes) onto the Maple Line skeleton; fill gaps from Mesh2Motion and KayKit; hand-key `check-phone`, `watch-train`, `shelter`, `board` and a bench `sit`. Export shared clips in one GLB so characters reference them by name.
5. **Crowd drawing.** Start with option 1 or 2; try InstancedMesh2 only if measurements say rigid parts are not enough.
6. **Records.** Add a character entry to the provenance manifest (next section) in the same commit as every exported file.

If the art direction later accepts an anime look, VRoid + three-vrm is the fastest route to expressive faces, with licences that allow commercial use.
